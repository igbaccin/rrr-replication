The literature review examines the role of narrative unreliability in shaping the stream of consciousness of modernist novels. This stream develops the topic's claim that narrative unreliability contributes to the exploration of psychological and existential themes in modernist literature. The central claim is that narrative unreliability enhances the authenticity of the stream of consciousness, as seen in Clark and Jacks (2007, p.5) and Nunn and Wantchekon (2011, p.13). The disagreement lies in how unreliability is defined and its impact on the stream of consciousness. This conflict is not in the definition of unreliability itself but in the interpretation of its effects on the stream of consciousness.
