What is the role of institutions in determining long-run economic growth? This question is central to understanding how structural and institutional frameworks shape economic outcomes over time. The literature presents a complex landscape of perspectives, with some arguing that institutions are key drivers of growth, while others emphasize the limitations of institutional theory in explaining economic development. The debate centers on whether institutional quality, structural constraints, or other factors play a more decisive role in fostering economic progress. Scholars like Acemoglu et al. (2001, p.19) and Ogilvie (2007, p.27) highlight the importance of institutional frameworks in shaping economic trajectories, while Austin (2008, p.2) critiques the oversimplification of causality in institutionalist accounts. The tension lies in reconciling the empirical evidence of institutional influence with the challenges of quantifying and generalizing these effects. The question remains unresolved, but the ongoing dialogue underscores the need for a nuanced understanding of how institutions interact with other economic and social variables to determine long-term growth.
