This claim is grounded in the shared evidence of (Ogilvie 2007, p.27), (North and Weingast 1989, p.2), and (North 1989, p.9), which collectively highlight how institutional frameworks, particularly those involving property rights, legal stability, and collective action, are essential for fostering economic progress. The interdependence of institutions, as emphasized by Ogilvie (2007, p.27), underscores the necessity of examining institutional frameworks as a whole rather than in isolation. The evidence from Acemoglu et al. (2001, p.19) and Acemoglu et al. These findings collectively establish that institutional structures are not only a driver of growth but also a foundational element in the process of economic evolution. Institutions, therefore, are not merely incidental to economic growth but are central to its emergence and sustainability.
