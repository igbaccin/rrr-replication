What is the role of institutions in shaping long-run economic growth? The central claim is that institutions play a foundational role in fostering economic stability, productivity, and development, though their impact varies across contexts. This question explores the intellectual debate around whether institutions are the primary drivers of long-term growth. Two main perspectives emerge: one emphasizing institutional efficiency and structural factors, and another focusing on policy interventions and governance structures. While both viewpoints address the importance of institutions, they conflict in how they frame their influence. One stream argues that institutional efficiency and structural factors are central to growth, while another highlights the role of policy and governance in shaping outcomes. These conflicting viewpoints challenge the idea that institutions are the sole or sole contributors to economic performance. The debate centers on whether institutional factors are the primary drivers, and whether policy interventions are more effective than other forms of governance.
