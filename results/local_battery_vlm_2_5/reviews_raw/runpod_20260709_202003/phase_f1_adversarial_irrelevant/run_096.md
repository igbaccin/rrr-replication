The role of institutions in shaping long-run economic growth is a contentious issue in economic literature, with scholars debating whether institutions are foundational or merely supportive of growth. Some scholars argue that institutions are the primary drivers of economic growth, while others contend that technological progress and social change are the key factors. This disagreement is not merely academic; it has significant implications for policy-making and economic development strategies. Understanding the role of institutions in economic growth can inform policies aimed at fostering sustainable development and addressing economic inequalities. The debate centers around whether institutions facilitate technological progress and social change or whether they are secondary to these factors. The resolution of this debate requires a nuanced understanding of how institutions interact with technological advancements and social dynamics to drive economic growth. Technological advancements are pivotal for long-term economic growth. Mokyr (2005, p.54) and Kuznets (1973, p.10) both highlight the primacy of technological progress in driving economic growth. Mokyr (2005, p.54) explains that technological progress relies on the accumulation and recombination of existing knowledge. This growth of useful knowledge involves new inventions and the efficient use of existing techniques, which can be transformed into novel applications through analogical thinking. Kuznets (1973, p.10) complements this view by noting that technological innovations are often responses to specific needs, and their mass application provides a positive feedback loop for further scientific and technological advancements. This interplay between technological progress and economic growth suggests that the accumulation of useful knowledge is a self-reinforcing process that drives long-term economic development. Mokyr (2005, p.54) further elaborates on the necessity of institutional progress to complement technological advancements. This institutional support is crucial for sustaining economic improvements, as it helps prevent rent-seeking behaviors that could erode the benefits of technological progress. Kuznets (1973, p.10) adds that the efficient use of innovations requires institutional adjustments and ideological changes that align societal values with economic goals. The role of institutions in facilitating technological progress underscores the importance of a supportive institutional framework in harnessing the full potential of technological advancements for economic growth.
