Nunn and Wantchekon [E0113] [E0025] argue that the slave trade in Africa led to a culture of mistrust that persists today, impacting contemporary levels of trust within African societies. This mistrust is a significant factor in the region's economic underdevelopment, as it impedes the formation of social capital and the development of institutions that support economic growth. The slave trade disrupted traditional social structures and created an environment where individuals were more likely to mistrust others, leading to a persistent culture of mistrust that has hindered economic development. Austin (2008) [E0026] notes that colonial administrations, in response to indigenous economic and political action, modified their institutional prescriptions. This adaptation, however, did not fully mitigate the mistrust fostered by the slave trade. The legacy of mistrust continues to affect economic outcomes, as it undermines the effectiveness of institutions and reduces the willingness of individuals to engage in cooperative economic activities. This mistrust, therefore, remains a significant barrier to economic growth in Africa.
