What is the role of institutions in determining long-run economic growth? This question lies at the heart of economic theory and historical inquiry, and it reflects a deep intellectual engagement with the mechanisms that shape development. While some scholars emphasize the importance of institutional quality as a driver of growth, others argue that institutions are more complex and context-specific, shaped by historical and cultural forces. The debate is not merely academic, it touches on the very nature of economic development, the sustainability of institutional frameworks, and the conditions under which institutions can foster growth. Some argue that institutions are the primary force behind long-term economic progress, while others contend that institutions are merely one of many factors, influenced by external conditions and historical trajectories. This review synthesizes scholarly perspectives on this issue, focusing on the role of institutions in shaping economic growth through their impact on productivity, governance, and social structures. The evidence suggests that institutions play a crucial role in determining growth, but the extent and nature of this role remain contested. Institutions, whether formal or informal, are essential for enabling economic development, as they provide the foundation for productive relationships, legal frameworks, and the accumulation of human capital. In this context, the interplay between institutional quality and economic outcomes is both significant and complex, reflecting the dynamic and evolving nature of economic systems. The paper highlights that institutions, such as those governing property rights and the rule of law, are crucial for fostering economic development. Similarly, North and Weingast (1989, p.2) argue that secure rights and economic growth are rare in seventeenth-century England, underscoring the importance of institutional stability in enabling growth. The institutional frameworks that emerge from political and economic processes are vital for sustaining long-term growth, as they provide the structural basis for economic development. The persistence of these institutions across different historical contexts indicates that they are not merely contingent on external factors but are deeply embedded in the fabric of economic systems. Institutions, therefore, are not only a response to economic conditions but also a driver of economic outcomes, shaping the trajectory of long-run growth through their influence on policy, governance, and the broader economic environment.
