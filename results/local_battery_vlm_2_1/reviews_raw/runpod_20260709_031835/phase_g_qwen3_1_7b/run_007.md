What is the role of institutions in determining long-run economic growth? This question is central to understanding how structural and institutional factors shape economic trajectories over time. The literature presents a complex web of competing views, with some arguing that institutions are key drivers of growth, while others emphasize the limited impact of institutions or the importance of other factors. The debate centers on whether institutions, through their influence on governance, resource allocation, and social norms, can create the conditions necessary for sustained economic development. The evidence suggests that institutions are not merely passive structures but active agents that shape economic outcomes through their ability to foster cooperation, reduce uncertainty, and promote innovation. The challenge lies in reconciling these differing perspectives and identifying the mechanisms through which institutions contribute to long-term economic growth.
