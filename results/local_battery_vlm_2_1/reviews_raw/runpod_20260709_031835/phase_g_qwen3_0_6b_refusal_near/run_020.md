The disagreement lies in differing positions on the causal relationship between institutions and economic outcomes. One perspective posits that central planning contributed to stagnation and inefficiency, while another argues that it facilitated economic growth through market mechanisms. This tension is evident in the literature, which synthesizes across sources to argue that institutions matter in shaping economic outcomes. Evidence supports that central planning, as a form of extractive institutions, led to divergent economic paths, suggesting that institutional features are key to understanding the human cost of industrialization.
