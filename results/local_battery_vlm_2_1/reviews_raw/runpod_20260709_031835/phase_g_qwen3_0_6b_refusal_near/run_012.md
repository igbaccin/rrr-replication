However, the argument that economic growth was a result of institutional factors remains contested. This disagreement is grounded in the evidence provided by Acemoglu et al. In contrast, the evidence from Temin (2002, p.16) suggests that the changes in institutions resulting from colonialism explain the reversal in relative income among former European colonies. This synthesis highlights the complex interplay between institutions and economic development, reinforcing the role of central planning in shaping the outcomes of forced industrialization under Stalin.
